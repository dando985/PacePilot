# General Fitness Foundations

This document is original PacePilot educational material based on the
public-health guidance listed in the Sources section. It provides general
educational information and is not individualized medical advice.

## Building a Weekly Routine

A sustainable fitness routine should fit the person's current ability,
schedule, and preferred activities. Someone starting from a low activity
level does not need to reach every long-term target immediately. Beginning
with manageable sessions and consistently repeating them is more useful than
starting with a schedule that cannot be maintained.

Adults can work toward at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic
activity per week, 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, or an equivalent
combination. Muscle-strengthening activities involving the major muscle
groups can be included on at least two days each week.

## Choosing Activities

General fitness does not require running. Aerobic activity can include
walking, cycling, swimming, dancing, or other activities that increase effort
and can be performed consistently.

Activities should match the person's current ability and interests. Enjoyable
and accessible activities are generally easier to repeat over time. Weekly
activity can also be divided into smaller sessions instead of being completed
all at once.

## Starting from a Low Baseline

Some activity is more beneficial than none. A beginner can start with a
duration and intensity that feels manageable and build consistency before
adding more demanding sessions.

PacePilot should avoid increasing several training variables simultaneously.
For example, a plan should not sharply increase session duration, weekly
frequency, and intensity during the same adjustment.

## Strength and Recovery

Aerobic activity and strength training serve different purposes and can both
be part of a balanced routine. Strength sessions should cover the major muscle
groups rather than focusing only on the muscles used in one preferred
activity.

Recovery time is part of the training schedule. Easier days and rest days give
the athlete an opportunity to recover between more demanding sessions.
Persistent pain, unusual exhaustion, or a meaningful decline in normal
function should not be treated as a signal to simply train harder.

## Individual Considerations

A generic plan cannot account for every medical condition, disability, injury,
or medication. Someone with a chronic health condition should discuss
appropriate activities and amounts with a qualified healthcare professional.
A previously inactive person should seek individualized guidance before
beginning vigorous exercise when appropriate.

PacePilot should provide educational guidance, not diagnose injuries or
replace professional medical care.
